To help tackle this issue, Rakuten launched a new service called Minna no Ticket (Everyone's Ticket). This platform serves as the official resale hub for two of Japan's major sports franchises: the Tohoku Rakuten Golden Eagles baseball team, and Rakuten's J.League soccer club Vissel Kobe, which recently clinched its fifth championship trophy.

The service is designed to solve a simple, but frustrating dilemma for two groups of fans: those wanting to attend a sold-out game and those needing to offload a ticket when life gets in the way.

Minna no Ticket uses blockchain technology to provide a highly secure and transparent ecosystem for peer-to-peer ticket transfers. Every transaction is recorded on an immutable digital ledger, neutralizing the risk of data tampering, ticket duplication or outright fraud. Fans can buy secondary tickets with total peace of mind knowing their purchase is genuine.

One of the platform's most significant innovations lies in its economic model. While predatory resellers on black markets siphon value away from the sports ecosystem, Rakuten's blockchain solution flips this dynamic. Every time a ticket changes hands on the platform, a portion of the revenue is routed back to the sports teams. Even when buying secondary tickets, fans are directly supporting their favorite teams.

Beating the black market with dynamic pricing
The platform also leverages a market-driven approach to ticket pricing. Many official resale services only offer a rigid system of fixed prices. This often pushes high-demand transactions back into unregulated, illegal channels where buyers are prepared to pay a massive premium.
regulated
Premium: 溢价

Minna no Ticket has opted to remove artificial price caps and floors. By allowing dynamic, market-driven pricing, the system effectively undercuts the appeal of illicit markets. Fans have the flexibility to list their tickets at a price that reflects true market demand, ensuring a fair exchange while keeping the transaction within a safe environment.
